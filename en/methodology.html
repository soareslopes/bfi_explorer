How it is calculated

Methodology

Five dimensions, twenty-five indicators, 278 municipalities, built entirely from open data.

The idea

The Bike Friendly Index assesses how friendly a municipality is to cycling as a mode of urban transport. It was built on the scientific literature about what drives cycling levels, with political commitment added on top, to capture how much each municipality is actually investing in cycling and in sustainable urban mobility.

Values are normalised on a 0 to 10 scale. The final index is a weighted mean of the five dimensions, and each dimension is itself a weighted mean of its indicators.

The weights changed in 2025

The 2025 edition rebalances the weights: it takes weight away from what the territory simply inherited, such as slope, and gives more to what depends on policy and behaviour, such as municipal spending and actual cycling levels.

Normalisation

The 2025 edition uses three normalisation regimes, depending on the nature of each indicator:

  • Benchmark against the observed maximum. Infrastructure, political commitment and cycling levels. The best municipality in the country sets the 10 and everyone else is measured against it.
  • Capped normative thresholds. The four density indicators. Above 3,000 people/km² in the urban area, 1,000 people/km² across the municipality, and 1,200 and 400 dwellings/km² respectively, further density is taken to add nothing more for cycling.
  • Min-max. The remaining slope and built environment indicators, for which no external benchmark exists. The average block length indicator is inverted, because shorter blocks favour cycling.

Sources

Known limitations

  • OpenStreetMap coverage is uneven, particularly in less densely populated areas. The BFI feeds information back into that database whenever it gathers complementary data.
  • Bike sharing is treated as a yes or no. There is no current, reliable data on the number of bikes, docks or territorial coverage of each scheme.
  • Political commitment measures contracted spending, not spending delivered nor the quality of the works. A single large contract can move a small municipality a long way up the ranking.
  • In low density municipalities, small denominators make per capita and per km² indicators volatile between editions.

Comparability across editions

The figures published in 2018 and in 2025 are not directly comparable. The comparison uses a recomputed series, applying the 2025 weights to the 2018 indicators.
